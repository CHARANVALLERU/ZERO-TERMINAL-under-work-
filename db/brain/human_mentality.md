# ZERO ENGINE — Human Mentality & Trader Psychology Framework

## Foundation Principle
Markets are not random. They are driven by the aggregated decisions of humans — each with identical cognitive weaknesses, fears, and greed. Understanding your own psychology IS the edge.

## THE BIG 8 COGNITIVE BIASES IN TRADING

### 1. FOMO — Fear Of Missing Out
- **Definition**: Entering a trade because you fear being left behind as price moves, not because your system triggered.
- **Triggers**: Watching a stock move 3%+ without you. News euphoria. Social media buzz.
- **Symptoms**: Late entries near tops. Over-sized positions. Abandoning your plan mid-session.
- **Neural Mechanism**: Dopamine release from seeing others profit activates the same reward circuits as gambling addiction.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Pre-define entry levels BEFORE the session. No entries without pre-planned triggers.
  - If you missed it, it wasn't yours. The market will give another setup.
  - Rule: "I do not buy breakouts more than 0.5% above my planned entry."
  - Log every FOMO entry. Review the P&L. The data will fix the behavior.

### 2. Loss Aversion
- **Definition**: The pain of a loss is psychologically 2.5x more powerful than the pleasure of an equivalent gain (Kahneman & Tversky).
- **Manifestation in Trading**:
  - Holding losing trades too long hoping to "get back to breakeven"
  - Cutting winners too early to "lock in profit" out of fear it reverses
  - Refusing to exit even when your system says to
- **The Asymmetry Problem**: You'd rather avoid a ₹500 loss than make a ₹1000 gain — this is irrational for trading profitability.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Pre-set stop losses BEFORE entry. Let the system execute. You don't decide.
  - Reframe: "Exiting at stop is executing my plan perfectly." Not a loss — a discipline win.
  - Track R-multiples, not rupee P&L. A -₹500 on a 1R trade with 3R winners is a profitable system.

### 3. Overconfidence Bias
- **Definition**: Overestimating the accuracy of your own analysis and predictions.
- **Dangerous Seasons**: After a streak of 3-5 winning trades. After a "perfect" read on a macro event.
- **Signs**: Increasing position size beyond your plan. Skipping confirmation signals. "This is obvious."
- **The Dunning-Kruger Effect**: Peak confidence correlates inversely with actual understanding in complex systems.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Position sizing is FIXED. No exceptions for "high conviction" trades.
  - Track your prediction accuracy. Numbers don't lie.
  - After 3 wins: deliberately slow down. Do not add to next trade.
  - Rule: "The market does not care about my conviction."

### 4. Revenge Trading
- **Definition**: Taking impulsive trades immediately after a loss to "recover" the lost capital.
- **Why It's Lethal**: Emotional state, not analysis, drives the decision. Risk controls are abandoned. Position sizes inflate.
- **The Spiral**: Loss → Revenge trade → Bigger loss → More revenge → Account blown.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Hard rule: After 2 consecutive losses or one loss > 1.5R → STOP for the day.
  - 15-minute cooling period mandatory after any loss exceeding ₹[define your number].
  - Write in the journal immediately after a loss: What was my reason? Did I follow the plan?
  - The market will be there tomorrow. Your capital won't if you revenge trade.

### 5. Confirmation Bias
- **Definition**: Seeking information that confirms your existing position and ignoring contradictory signals.
- **In Trading**: You're long Nifty → you see bullish news everywhere. The bearish signals become "noise."
- **Why Dangerous**: Leads to holding losing trades based on selective information processing.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Actively seek the OPPOSITE thesis before entering. "Why would I be wrong?"
  - Inversion technique: "If this trade goes to stop, what did the market know that I didn't?"
  - Set price-based exits, not narrative-based exits.

### 6. Anchoring Bias
- **Definition**: Over-relying on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making decisions.
- **Trading Form**: "Nifty was at 24,500 yesterday, so 24,200 is cheap." This ignores current context.
- **Symptoms**: Holding losing trades to "get back" to entry price. Setting profit targets at round numbers with no technical basis.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Current price is the only true price. History is for analysis, not for expectation.
  - Base entries and exits on current structure: support, resistance, ZERO matrix levels.
  - Remove your entry price from your trading platform display during live trades.

### 7. Herding / Social Proof
- **Definition**: Following the crowd because you assume the collective wisdom is correct.
- **Trading Triggers**: Twitter/X consensus. WhatsApp group tips. Analyst upgrades. "Everyone is buying this."
- **The Paradox**: When herding is maximum, the move is usually near exhaustion. The crowd exits simultaneously → crash.
- **Mitigation**:
  - Your system is your religion. External opinions are noise.
  - When everyone agrees, take the contrarian assessment seriously.
  - Social media consumption: zero during market hours.

### 8. Recency Bias
- **Definition**: Overweighting recent events and extrapolating them indefinitely into the future.
- **After a Bull Run**: "Markets always go up." After a crash: "Markets are going to zero."
- **After a Winning Streak**: Increase risk because "I've figured it out."
- **Mitigation**:
  - Review performance across all market cycles, not just recent ones.
  - The regime will change. Strategies that worked recently may underperform.
  - ZERO engine uses historical distributions, not recent-only data, for this reason.

## THE DISCIPLINE FRAMEWORK

### Pre-Market Ritual (ZERO Mental Protocol)
1. **Check ZERO predictions** → Note the range. This is your universe for the day.
2. **Define bias** → Bullish or Bearish? Based on ZERO signal, not gut.
3. **Set 3 price alerts** → Entry trigger, stop-loss, target.
4. **Score your mental state** (1-10): Energy, focus, emotional neutrality.
   - Score < 6 → Trade size = 50% of normal
   - Score < 4 → No trading today
5. **Write ONE sentence**: "Today I will focus on ___."

### During Session Rules
- **No chart addiction**: Set alerts, walk away. Check at intervals.
- **One trade maximum per instrument per session** (for developing traders)
- **No trading in first 15 minutes** (9:15-9:30 AM): Noise is maximum, signal is minimum.
- **No trading in last 30 minutes** (3:00-3:30 PM): Institutional squaring volatility.

### Post-Market Review (MANDATORY)
Rate each of the following on 1-10:
- Did I follow my plan?
- Did I manage risk correctly?
- Was I emotionally neutral?
- Did I avoid all bias behaviors?
Average = **discipline score**. Log this in ZERO Brain.

## THE MENTAL MODELS LIBRARY

### First Principles Thinking
- Break the trade down to fundamental truths: Where is price? Where is the trend? What does the data say?
- Ignore narratives. Focus on structure.

### Inversion
- Before any trade: "What would make this trade fail?"
- Before sizing up: "What is the worst-case scenario?"
- This is ZERO's built-in: always check the downside range first.

### Probabilistic Thinking
- No trade is certain. You are always working with probabilities.
- A good trade can lose. A bad trade can win. You cannot control the outcome — only the process.
- Evaluate systems over 100 trades, not 1.

### The Two-Decision Framework
- Decision 1 (Before trade): Entry, size, stop, target — FULLY DEFINED.
- Decision 2 (During trade): NONE. The system decides.

### Antifragility
- A strong trader system benefits from volatility, not breaks under it.
- High-volatility days = more ZERO accuracy value → embrace them, don't fear them.
